# Goals

Goal management and achievement acknowledgement are owner-only. Reads use the journal's current SQL visibility rules, including profile privacy and accepted friendships. APIs authenticate members and return private/no-store responses. Goal progress never exposes entry notes.

Volume counts distinct dated original sends in the chosen discipline, either at an exact grade or at that grade and harder. Repeats and undated sends do not contribute. First-grade milestones complete at the chosen grade or harder; a known undated send or a send before the goal window at that grade or harder prevents creating the milestone. Eligibility is accepted when saved and rechecked only when changing the milestone grade, discipline, or start date, or converting another goal into a milestone. Later imports do not revoke that eligibility. Completion still follows qualifying dated logs inside the goal window and reverses if those logs are removed. Training counts training entries. Climbing days count distinct session dates. New areas count first logged visits to direct climb areas, not ancestors or repeat visits.

Dates are civil dates in each goal's saved timezone. Weeks start Monday; months and years are calendar periods. Presets include existing logs within the window. Custom ranges preserve explicit start/end dates. Grade milestones cannot recur. Training supports week/month/custom deadlines and weekly/monthly recurrence; other goal types also support yearly recurrence.

Active contains unfinished goals, current recurring goals, and missed one-time goals awaiting a decision. Completed one-time goals appear in History immediately. Recurring goals keep an active slot even when the current period is met; their past periods appear in History automatically. Archive remains available for missed one-time goals awaiting a decision. Recurring goal creation and editing include an optional last inclusive civil date, with No end date as the default. Active recurring goals show No end date or their scheduled end date beneath the current period date; the owner uses Edit in the actions menu to set, change, or remove it. The final period keeps its full target and counts only logs through that date; later periods are not generated. The routine stays active through that date, then moves to History and frees its slot. The end date can be changed while it is current or future; ended routines cannot be edited or restarted. Create a new goal to start again. End dates cannot be set in the past. Completed one-time goals remain in History and friends’ feeds and do not occupy active slots. Missed goals remain there for one calendar month from the first day after their deadline, or until acted on, and do not occupy one of the five active slots. Try again opens a prefilled form with fresh dates; saving atomically creates a new goal and archives the original, while cancellation or failed creation leaves it awaiting a decision. Archive goal moves the original to History immediately. After the decision month, History shows the original automatically. While awaiting a decision, missed goals retain Edit/Delete alongside Try again and Archive goal. Editing retains the original dates unless explicitly changed. Missed goals in History cannot be retried or archived again, and resolved originals cannot be edited. The five-active-goal limit is enforced in the write statement, including concurrent requests. Edits that remain inactive or do not increase active count are allowed at capacity. Corrected logs can reactivate an unexpired goal; all reactivated goals remain visible and new creation is blocked at capacity.

History includes met and missed results and groups each goal once per selected year, retaining recurring history even after conversion to one-time. Only met results count as achievements. One-time results belong to their completion year, or deadline year when missed; recurring periods belong to their start year. Cadence changes preserve ended periods and apply the new calendar window to the current attempt. Overlapping old/new windows may both include a log, but the goal is counted once in the yearly total. Snapshots retain the original type, grade, target, cadence, timezone and hashtag filters; progress remains derived from logs.

The owner-only Progress area has separate Goals, Projects and Analytics pages, with Projects split into Open and Sent sub-tabs. Both project views list only climbs the owner pinned; a pin moves between them when a send is logged or removed, and is never created or deleted on the climber's behalf. The Goals page derives current progress from logs and shares that aggregate between both views. Journal and Projects do not duplicate goals. History pagination uses stable calendar bounds: three months for weekly routines, twelve for monthly routines, five years for yearly routines. Contribution requests identify the period by start and end dates so overlapping cadences cannot be confused. All reads recheck current visibility. Period counts are calculated live, including after moderation changes and calendar rollover.

Training and climb-volume goals may select up to eight hashtags in the editor when the owner has existing journal tags. An existing goal with selected tags keeps its tag editor even if those tags no longer occur in the journal. Saved filters on older goals of other types continue to apply when those goals are edited. Every selected hashtag must appear on the contributing entry; no selection means all otherwise eligible entries count. Filters use normalized, exact tags and apply to progress, completion, contribution details and active-slot counting. First-grade eligibility and whether an area is new still follow the lifetime history rules above. Editing entry tags can revoke a completion. Recurring snapshots retain the filter used for each ended period.

The `goal_completions` table persists feed events by goal, period start and cadence, with a stable event ID, completion date and saved period definition. Feed titles are formatted at read time by the same goalTitle helper used in Goals. The feed displays the last reconciled events. Owner writes that can affect goal progress—logging, editing/deleting sessions or dated sends, importing and goal mutations—and owner Goals visits schedule reconciliation with Next.js `after()`, after the response. Reads of Goals and contribution details use live counts rather than a stored progress cache.

A moderator’s climb edit or merge does not refresh affected owners’ events. Those events remain as last reconciled until each owner’s next relevant write or Goals visit. A failed after-response refresh has the same behavior and never rejects a saved log. There are no invalidation triggers or scheduled recovery worker. Future-dated completions stay unpublished until a later owner write or Goals visit reconciles them on or after the goal’s local completion date.

Reconciliation recomputes all of the owner’s periods from current database rows in one atomic batch, updates completion events, and records achievements plus legacy initialization together. Notices recheck the live period, stored completion and acknowledgement before returning. A friend’s feed visit reads stored events and never detects or acknowledges owner achievements. Backdated completions retain their session date, and corrections remove invalid events at the next owner reconciliation. Archived goals keep their feed events; deleting a goal cascades to its events. Feed reads enforce current Journal and goals visibility, profile privacy and accepted friendships, so stale events cannot bypass a privacy change or survive goal deletion. Goal events are excluded from Sends-only feeds.

Achievement detection is independent of the contributing log date. New goals start detection immediately; pre-feature history is baselined silently on the first reconciliation scheduled by owner activity. Unseen achievements remain pending across days and devices, including backdated logs. View or dismiss acknowledges the displayed goal/period keys together. Corrected/deleted logs remove invalid pending results, while acknowledgement remains durable so deleting and relogging does not celebrate the same period again.
