# Goals

Goal management and achievement acknowledgement are owner-only. Reads use the journal's current SQL visibility rules, including profile privacy and accepted friendships. APIs authenticate members and return private/no-store responses. Goal progress never exposes entry notes.

Volume counts distinct dated original sends in the chosen discipline, either at an exact grade or at that grade and harder. Repeats and undated sends do not contribute. First-grade milestones complete at the chosen grade or harder; a known undated send or a send before the goal window at that grade or harder prevents creating the milestone. Eligibility is accepted when saved and rechecked only when changing the milestone grade, discipline, or start date, or converting another goal into a milestone. Later imports do not revoke that eligibility. Completion still follows qualifying dated logs inside the goal window and reverses if those logs are removed. Training counts training entries. Climbing days count distinct session dates. New areas count first logged visits to direct climb areas, not ancestors or repeat visits.

Dates are civil dates in each goal's saved timezone. Weeks start Monday; months and years are calendar periods. Presets include existing logs within the window. Custom ranges preserve explicit start/end dates. Grade milestones cannot recur. Training supports week/month/custom deadlines and weekly/monthly recurrence; other goal types also support yearly recurrence.

Active contains unfinished goals, current recurring goals, unarchived finished one-time goals, and missed one-time goals awaiting a decision. Recurring goals keep an active slot even when the current period is met; their past periods appear in History automatically. Archive applies to one-time goals. Recurring goal creation and editing include an optional last inclusive civil date, with No end date as the default. End routine is a shortcut for scheduling that date, defaulting to the current period’s end. Before the routine has ended, the editor can change or remove the date. The final period keeps its full target and counts only logs through that date; later periods are not generated. The routine stays active through that date, then moves to History and frees its slot. The end date can be changed while it is current or future; ended routines cannot be edited or restarted. Create a new goal to start again. End dates cannot be set in the past. Finished goals have no count or age limit and do not occupy active slots. Archive goal dismisses a finish from Active while retaining it in History and friends’ feeds. Missed goals remain there for one calendar month from the first day after their deadline, or until acted on, and do not occupy one of the five active slots. Try again opens a prefilled form with fresh dates; saving atomically creates a new goal and archives the original, while cancellation or failed creation leaves it awaiting a decision. Archive goal moves the original to History immediately. After the decision month, History shows the original automatically. While awaiting a decision, missed goals retain Edit/Delete alongside Try again and Archive goal. Editing retains the original dates unless explicitly changed. Missed goals in History cannot be retried or archived again, and resolved originals cannot be edited. The five-active-goal limit is enforced in the write statement, including concurrent requests. Edits that remain inactive or do not increase active count are allowed at capacity. Corrected logs can reactivate an unexpired goal; all reactivated goals remain visible and new creation is blocked at capacity.

History includes met and missed results and groups each goal once per selected year, retaining recurring history even after conversion to one-time. Only met results count as achievements. One-time results belong to their completion year, or deadline year when missed; recurring periods belong to their start year. Cadence changes preserve ended periods and apply the new calendar window to the current attempt. Overlapping old/new windows may both include a log, but the goal is counted once in the yearly total. Snapshots retain the original type, grade, target, cadence, timezone and hashtag filters; progress remains derived from logs.

The owner-only Progress area has separate Goals, Open Projects, and Analytics pages. The Goals page shares projected period progress between both views and repairs stale projections when needed. Journal and Open Projects do not duplicate goals. History pagination uses stable calendar bounds: three months for weekly routines, twelve for monthly routines, five years for yearly routines. Contribution requests identify the period by start and end dates so overlapping cadences cannot be confused. All reads recheck visibility and SQL freshness. Stored period counts are reused until relevant source changes or calendar rollover require reconciliation.

Goals may select up to eight hashtags. Every selected hashtag must appear on the contributing entry; no selection means all otherwise eligible entries count. Filters use normalized, exact tags and apply to progress, completion, contribution details and active-slot counting. First-grade eligibility and whether an area is new still follow the lifetime history rules above. Editing entry tags can revoke a completion. Recurring snapshots retain the filter used for each ended period.

The `goal_progress` table stores counts and completion dates for every evaluated period, including unfinished and missed periods. Source triggers mark potentially affected goals dirty using current and historical date windows; earlier visits also invalidate new-area goals. Changing a recurring end date invalidates progress; logs after it do not invalidate that routine’s counts. Metadata-only refresh fields and archive-token changes do not invalidate counts. Clean reads join current definitions and privacy to projected counts in the same SQL snapshot. Missing, dirty, or stale-date projections trigger bounded owner repair; visitors fall back to live authorized counts without initializing notices. Calendar rollover adds new periods and updates eligibility, while an older request cannot move the evaluated date backward. Refresh aggregates dirty or missing periods once, derives events and acknowledgements, then clears dirty state last in the same atomic batch.

The `goal_completions` table persists feed events by goal, period start and cadence, with a stable event ID, completion date and goal title. Logging, editing/deleting sessions or sends, importing, goal mutations and relevant climb moderation refresh this projection from qualifying logs before returning. Database triggers first invalidate affected events inside the source write. A failed derived refresh does not reject an already saved log; invalid events remain hidden until a later write or owner Goals read repairs them. Refresh statements recompute against current database rows and record achievements plus legacy initialization in one atomic batch. This prevents stale reads from creating phantom notices and concurrent logging from turning baselined history into new achievements. Notices recheck the current completion and acknowledgement before they are returned. Owner Goals reads also reconcile existing history. The feed reads stored events directly; a friend’s visit does not detect achievements. Backdated completions retain their session date, and corrections remove invalid events. Archived goals keep their feed events; deleting a goal cascades to its events. Feed reads recheck current Journal and goals visibility, profile privacy and accepted friendships, and never acknowledge owner notices. Goal events are excluded from Sends-only feeds.

Achievement detection is independent of the contributing log date. New goals start detection immediately; pre-feature history is baselined silently on the owner's first visit. Unseen achievements remain pending across days and devices, including backdated logs. View or dismiss acknowledges the displayed goal/period keys together. Corrected/deleted logs remove invalid pending results, while acknowledgement remains durable so deleting and relogging does not celebrate the same period again.
