# Trips

## Day rows and analytics

A trip is a date range with a name, a description, Markdown notes and a link to a shared Google Photos album (migrations 0049 and 0051). It has no entries of its own: its sends and journal entries are the ones dated within the range. Trips live at `/users/{id}/trips/{tripId}`, with analytics at `/analytics` under that. The trip page shows the album, the notes and a card for each day with something logged, and has no tabs or filters. Each card's header names the day, lists the crags its rows were at (each climb's own area name, joined by a middle dot, no ancestors) and counts what it lists.

The Sends tab's sort-direction arrow (`SortDirectionButton`, ascending for oldest first) at the end of the heading line orders them oldest or newest first. The server always sends the days oldest first and `TripDayOrderProvider` reverses them in the browser, so switching fetches nothing; it replaces the current React Router entry with `?order=newest`, keeping the rest of the query, and a page opened with it (`parseTripDayOrder`) starts in that order. Rows within a day keep the order they were logged. Under each day, `groupTripDays` (`frontend/src/lib/trip-days.ts`) interleaves sends with journal entries in the order they were logged, and a send takes the place of its ascent entry, which only repeats it; other entries for that climb the same day, such as failed sessions, stay. Each day header counts its rows the way their chips name them (sends, repeats, sessions, training).

Readers without journal access get the sends alone. The page reads at most `TRIP_DAYS_LIMIT` sends and entries each; a longer trip shows its latest complete days (or, if a read reaches only one day, that day in part) and links to the Journal and Sends tabs. Apart from `order`, URL params are ignored, and `withTripWindow` always applies the trip's dates, so neither `?dateFrom=` nor `?order=` can change the range. The entry and send counts link to the Journal and Sends tabs filtered to the trip's dates (`tripLogbookHref`), and the same line ends with a link to the trip's analytics once something is logged.

Trip analytics reuse `AnalyticsDashboard` with `TRIP_ANALYTICS_LAYOUT`, a fixed layout with tiles for sends, hardest, flash, onsight (rope only) and days out, in that order so the share image's default first four include onsight on rope trips, and the grade pyramid. Tiles and charts that compare months or years are left out.

## Audience and notes

Trips have their own audience, `user.trip_visibility` (Only me, Friends or Members; Friends by default for every account, migration 0055), set under Account → Privacy. It is independent of the journal and commentary audiences: it decides who can open the trips without a trip link and nothing else. Trip queries apply `trips_visible_sql` to member reads. Profile access determines whether the Trips tab is visible and whether the viewer can open the list and trip pages. A private profile still overrides it. Anyone the trip audience allows can open the trip, its sends and its analytics. The profile share link does not open trips.

Journal and trip access come from one `get_profile_access` read per request. Anyone allowed by `can_read_journal` also gets the journal entries under each day, entry counts, day counts and tagged friends; the entries are read through `get_journal_page`, which applies `journal_visible_sql` again. Notes are visible to the owner and their accepted friends, and to the trip's link. The journal audience doesn't apply to notes. These checks are in the SQL in `backend/db/src/trips.rs`. Journal counts are null and `hasNotes` is 0 when the viewer lacks access, and `member_notes` checks the viewer’s permission again in its own query.

## Descriptions and albums

The description is one line of up to `MAX_TRIP_DESCRIPTION` characters, visible to anyone who can open the trip. Album photos are also visible to anyone who can open the trip. Google has no embed and blocks iframes, so `trip_album::load` reads the album page for photo URLs, follows redirects only within Google Photos, and caches the result in the Cache API for an hour. Photos aren't stored or proxied. The viewer's browser loads them from Google with no referrer, because a trip link's URL is its token. A video in the album (`album_photos` reads its length from the item's data) plays in place: its poster frame is the photo URL, and `=m22` on that URL redirects to a 720p MP4 stream that needs no Google session.

Google refuses that stream when any referrer is sent, and a `video` element can't set a referrer policy, so `TripAlbumVideo` renders the player in a sandboxed `srcdoc` iframe whose own `<meta name="referrer">` is `no-referrer`. At rest the tile is the poster with the length; a tap or click anywhere plays it and turns the browser's controls on. The controls are off at rest because they swallow taps on a small tile. Nothing loads until play. If the stream errors, the poster links to the album.

## Share links

A trip link is the one way to show a trip to someone outside the trip audience, signed in or not. `trip_share_links` (migration 0050, emptied by 0056 so links from the first version, removed in #353, stay dead) holds one row per trip, keyed by a `lower(hex(randomblob(16)))` token that is the whole of `/trips/{token}`, with its analytics at `/trips/{token}/analytics`. It follows the project link in every rule: no audience; the share predicate in `backend/db/src/trips.rs` (not expired, owner not private) inside every read; `shared_trip` resolving the token to hidden, expired or visible with the trip, its counts and its notes in one read; `trips::share` checking ownership and privacy inside the INSERT, keeping the token when renewing a live link but making a new one once it has expired, so a link the owner let lapse stays dead; `trips::unshare` deleting the row, without the write rate limit, since revoking only reduces exposure; the composite foreign key deleting the link with its trip; `trip_share_revoke_on_private` deleting every row when a profile goes private; the metadata handler running the same predicate, so a dead link previews as nothing; and a `/trips/` terms exemption.

The owner's trip page has the Share button (`TripShare` over `ShareLinkDialog`, reading `trips::owner_share`). `trips::owner_share` returns the link only while it is live, on the same `datetime('now')` comparison as the shared page, so the button reads Shared only when the page would open, and on a private profile it says sharing is off instead of offering a link. The owner opening their own link, live or expired, is redirected to their trip page, where the dialog renews it. The page shows the trip the way the owner's own page shows it: the header, album, notes, every day with its sends and journal entries, and the analytics, overriding the owner's journal and commentary audiences for that trip.

Each send, journal and analytics query uses `TripReader::window` in its WHERE, which checks the token, its expiry, the owner's privacy and the trip's current dates in the statement that selects the rows (current on purpose: a link shares the trip as it evolves, so moving or extending its dates widens what the link shows), and selects with the owner as the viewer so commentary and videos match the owner's view. What it leaves out is everything that would go further than the trip: the trips list, the Journal and Sends links on the counts and the long-trip notice, hashtag links into the Journal (`TripDays`' `forLink`), the owner's controls, and tagged friends (shared journal queries omit companions, and the trip’s companion list is empty), who never saw the dialog. Nor does any trip or entry id: client components on the page are given none (`TripStatusChip` gets the dates, `TripNotes` gets `tripId` only to edit, `JournalCompanions` and `TripCompanions` render only with someone to name), and on the link's page day rows are keyed by climb id or position. The owner's page keys journal rows by entry id, so open dialogs stay with their entry.

The consent is the dialog's "Anyone with the link sees this trip: its notes, photos, journal entries, sends and analytics" (`TRIP_SHARE_SENTENCE`), so anything added to `SharedTrip` changes it first. Unlike the profile link, a trip link records no referral on sign-up, like a project link.

## Writes and companions

Only the owner can create, edit or delete a trip or its notes. Actions scope every write by `user_id`. Tagged friends use `trip_companions` (migration 0051), which has the same columns, guards, ten-tag cap and self-removal tombstone as `journal_companions`. The trip query returns them to anyone who can read the owner's journal, and `trips::save` writes them in the same batch as the trip. If the trip insert is skipped because the user is at their trip limit, `last_insert_rowid()` is stale, so the tag insert also checks `changes()`.
